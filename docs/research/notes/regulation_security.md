## Q1. KYC/AML, sanctions, FATF R16 (payment transparency) and FX paperwork: what must an agent that initiates cross-border payments do?

### Takeaway
FATF rewrote Recommendation 16 in June 2025 ("Payment transparency"): more complete originator/beneficiary data, explicit anti-fraud/error purpose, and "alignment checks" (name/account matching) for cross-border payments, with countries expected to implement by end-2030. The legal responsibility sits with the regulated institution that receives the customer's instruction, so the agent's job is to deliver a complete, verified, screened, purpose-documented instruction to that institution and never to bypass its checks; for China/Vietnam outbound student flows, FX-quota and purpose documentation (admission letter, tuition invoice) is the real friction the agent can automate.

### Cited Findings
- [F2] FATF approved the revised R16 at its June 2025 Plenary and published it on 18 June 2025; R16 is renamed "Payment transparency" and its purpose is extended beyond terrorist financing to money laundering and predicate offences such as fraud; it adds "tools to protect against fraud and error" — [FATF update page](https://www.fatf-gafi.org/en/publications/Fatfrecommendations/update-Recommendation-16-payment-transparency-june-2025.html) (page returned 403 to the fetcher; content via search summary); [Dudkowiak](https://www.dudkowiak.com/?p=14584)
- [F2] For peer-to-peer cross-border payments above USD/EUR 1,000 the sender's name, address and date of birth must be included; FIs must adopt technologies such as tools to verify recipient banking information; card purchases of goods/services stay exempt from full R16; "the payment chain begins with the institution that receives the customer's instruction"; changes to be implemented by end-2030 — [A&O Shearman FinReg](https://finreg.aoshearman.com/FATF-updates-standards-on-payment-transparency)
- [F2] The revised R16 introduces "alignment checks" to detect misdirected payments and aims to improve content/quality of originator/beneficiary information — [Regulation Tomorrow, Feb 2025](https://www.regulationtomorrow.com/2025/02/fatf-second-consultation-on-revisions-to-r-16-and-inr-16/)
- [F2] After the Oct 2025 Plenary FATF published a methodology annex for assessing the revised R16; on 24 June 2026 FATF opened consultation on draft guidance for the revised R16 (guidance does not change obligations) — [FATF page via search](https://www.fatf-gafi.org/en/publications/Fatfrecommendations/update-Recommendation-16-payment-transparency-june-2025.html); [Regulation Tomorrow, Jun 2026](https://www.regulationtomorrow.com/2026/06/fatf-consults-on-guidance-to-increase-payment-transparency/)
- [F2] China: individuals have a USD 50,000 annual FX purchase quota (raised from USD 20,000 in 2007; official SAFE page is from 2007); amounts above quota for study need extra proof (admission letter/I-20 plus school invoice per a 2026 secondary blog); an older CCB page (2009) describes tuition-statement-based purchase rules — current tuition-specific thresholds NOT verified — [SAFE 2007](https://www.safe.gov.cn/en/2007/0105/819.html); [uni2study](https://www.uni2study.com/articles/chinas-foreign-exchange-controls-and-paying-us-tuition); [CCB](https://ccb.com/en/foreignexchange/v3/20090616_1245117696.html?ptId=7)
- [F2] Vietnam: Circular 20/2022/TT-NHNN (effective 15 Feb 2023) governs one-way transfers abroad by residents; study abroad and medical treatment are permitted purposes; the amount is based on the foreign institution's cost notice; documentation requirements are delegated to each authorized bank — [Vietanlaw](https://vietanlaw.com/limitation-of-foreign-currency-transferring-abroad-in-vietnam); [LuatVietnam (Circular text)](https://english.luatvietnam.vn/circular-no-20-2022-tt-nhnn-guiding-activities-of-one-way-money-transfer-abroad-from-vietnam-and-240377-doc1.html)
- [F2] PBOC data measures (effective 1 May 2025) say processors must not circumvent export rules "through data splitting or transformation" (data rule, but shows regulator's anti-splitting mindset) — [HSF Kramer](https://www.hsfkramer.com/notes/data/2025-posts/chinas-financial-institutions-face-new-data-security-mandate)

### Inferences
- [I] What the agent must do before any outbound payment (checklist the judges will probe): (1) bind the instruction to a KYC'd account holder at the bank/PSP (agent never becomes the "originator"); (2) assemble complete structured originator + beneficiary data (name, account/IBAN, address, DOB for P2P >1,000) ready for the R16 era; (3) run name-account alignment / Verification of Payee before authorisation; (4) sanctions screening of beneficiary name, bank, country and purpose text against UN/EU/OFAC/China lists (bank does the authoritative check; agent pre-screens and stops on potential hits); (5) attach purpose code and supporting documents (admission letter, tuition invoice, relationship proof for family allowance); (6) check remaining FX quota and never split payments to stay under thresholds (anti-structuring rule in the policy engine); (7) show fee/FX/amount-received disclosures and obtain strong authentication from the user; (8) log everything.
- [I] For the tuition-autopilot and family-allowance ideas the policy engine should hard-code "no structuring": deny sequences of payments to the same beneficiary that sum above the quota/threshold within the period unless the documented path is used.
- [I] Positioning line for judges: "The agent is a preparer and initiator under a mandate; the bank remains the regulated originator. We never weaken its KYC/AML; we feed it cleaner, earlier, documented data."

## Q2. Consumer protection: remittance disclosures/cancellation, APP fraud reimbursement, Verification of Payee, PSD3/PSR, strong customer authentication

### Takeaway
Three live regimes define what "safe agent payments" look like: US Regulation E remittance rules (pre-payment disclosure, 30-minute cancellation, error resolution), the UK mandatory APP-scam reimbursement (since 7 Oct 2024, cap £85,000, ~85–88% of value reimbursed), and EU Verification of Payee (euro area since 9 Oct 2025) plus the agreed PSD3/PSR package (impersonation-fraud refunds, fraudster-modified payments treated as unauthorised, spending limits, human support not only chatbots). An agent that executes payments must reproduce these protections natively: show disclosures, verify payee, allow cancellation, and step-up on scam signals.

### Cited Findings
- [F] US Remittance Transfer Rule lives in Reg E §1005.31 (disclosures), §1005.33 (error resolution), §1005.34 (cancellation and refund); CFPB proposed a narrow disclosure amendment on 20 Sep 2024 and on 12 May 2025 withdrew several guidance documents incl. Bulletin 2012-08 on rule implementation — [CFPB Remittance Transfer Rule page](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/compliance/remittance-transfer-rule/)
- [F2] The rule gives senders the right to cancel certain transfers within 30 minutes of payment with a refund — [American Banker](https://www.americanbanker.com/payments/opinion/the-cfpbs-turning-up-the-heat-on-remittance-companies)
- [PK] Pre-payment disclosure must show amount, fees, taxes, exchange rate and amount to be received; a receipt adds date of availability and error/cancellation rights; senders have 180 days to report errors — [eCFR 12 CFR 1005 Subpart B](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-12/chapter-X/part-1005/subpart-B) (not re-fetched)
- [F2] UK mandatory APP reimbursement started 7 Oct 2024 (PSR Specific Direction 20): Faster Payments victims reimbursed up to £85,000 per claim (cut from £415,000; aligned to FSCS limit; PSR said 99.8% of cases fall below), cost split 50:50 between sending and receiving PSPs; consumers must report within 13 months; "consumer standard of caution" exception — [Which?](https://www.which.co.uk/news/article/new-fraud-protections-watered-down-at-the-last-minute-following-pressure-from-payments-industry-aYAvI0K06plD); [Bird & Bird](https://cm.twobirds.com/en/insights/2024/uk/new-uk-app-fraud-rules); [Bratby Law](https://bratby.law/app-fraud-reimbursement-review/)
- [F2] PSR dashboard (updated 11 Jun 2026, covering 7 Oct 2024–31 Dec 2025): 88% (£173m) of money lost to reimbursable APP scams returned Oct 2024–Sep 2025; Q4 2025 value reimbursed 85% (£70.2m); £215m reimbursed in 2025; ~83% of claims closed within five business days; ~3% rejected for lack of customer caution; UK Finance reported 65% for 2024 (not like-for-like) — [PSR APP scams reimbursement dashboard](https://www.psr.org.uk/information-for-consumers/app-scams-reimbursement-dashboard/); [FStech](https://fstech.co.uk/fst/PSR_Reveals_88_Of_Money_Lost_To_APP_Scams_Reimbursed.php)
- [F2] An independent review of the first year began Oct 2025, findings due 2026/27, testing the £85k cap, 50/50 split and consumer-caution exception — [Bratby Law](https://bratby.law/app-fraud-reimbursement-review/)
- [F2] EU Instant Payments Regulation (EU) 2024/886: from 9 Oct 2025 euro-area PSPs must offer Verification of Payee before authorising any credit transfer (instant and standard); non-euro EU PSPs by 9 Jul 2027 (one KPMG piece says 1 Jan 2027 — conflict); outcomes "match / close match (correct name shown) / no match (warning) / verification not possible"; if the payer proceeds after a warning, PSP liability is limited; EPC VoP scheme rulebook live 5 Oct 2025 — [ECB IPR page](https://www.ecb.europa.eu/paym/retail/instant_payments/html/instant_payments_regulation.er.html); [McCann FitzGerald](https://www.mccannfitzgerald.com/knowledge/financial-services-regulation/are-you-vop-to-go-prepare-for-verification-of-payee-by-october-2025); [KPMG](https://kpmg.com/de/en/home/insights/2025/10/verification-of-payee.html)
- [F2] PSD3/PSR: provisional political agreement 27 Nov 2025; final compromise texts published 23 Apr 2026 (COREPER endorsed 22 Apr 2026) — [William Fry](https://www.williamfry.com/knowledge/psr-psd3-eu-payment-services-legislation-is-agreed/); [Clifford Chance, Apr 2026](https://www.cliffordchance.com/content/dam/cliffordchance/briefings/2026/04/impact-of-psd3-are-you-ready.pdf)
- [F2] PSR content: PSP must refund in full when a scammer impersonates a PSP employee and tricks the customer into approving a payment, conditional on police report and informing the PSP; a transaction initiated or changed by a fraudster is treated as unauthorised (PSP liable in full); PSPs must check payee name vs unique identifier and refuse on discrepancy (note: stricter than IPR's "warn and let payer decide" — sources describe differently); PSPs must ensure SCA, risk assessment, offer spending limits and blocking measures; receiving PSP must freeze suspicious transactions; online platforms liable to PSPs if they fail to remove notified fraudulent content; users must have access to human customer support, not only chatbots — [William Fry](https://www.williamfry.com/knowledge/psr-psd3-eu-payment-services-legislation-is-agreed/)
- [E] OJ publication of PSD3/PSR was expected mid/H2 2026 and is NOT confirmed as of this research; application about 21 months after entry into force (~H1 2028), with PSR VoP provisions later (~27 months) — [Clifford Chance](https://www.cliffordchance.com/content/dam/cliffordchance/briefings/2026/04/impact-of-psd3-are-you-ready.pdf); [Gr4vy](https://gr4vy.com/posts/psd3-and-psr-explained-what-european-merchants-need-to-know-for-2026-and-beyond/); [Open Banking Tracker](https://openbankingtracker.com/guides/psd3-psr-readiness)
- [PK] Under PSD2 RTS on SCA (Delegated Reg. (EU) 2018/389, Art. 5 "dynamic linking"), the authentication code must be specific to the amount and payee the payer agreed to; any change invalidates it — [EUR-Lex 2018/389](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg_del/2018/389/oj) (not re-fetched)
- [F2] UK: under PSRs 2017, payment initiation requires explicit customer consent designed for humans; liability allocation under regulation 76 for agentic products is described as unclear by commentators; FCA's 2026 Payments Regulatory Priorities (25 Mar 2026) say it is working with HM Treasury to "modernise and future-proof" payments regulation incl. whether rules need to change for agentic AI payments — [Linklaters](https://techinsights.linklaters.com/post/102mo0a/uk-payments-priorities-for-2026-include-agentic-ai-and-stablecoins); [Bratby Law (search summary; page 403)](https://bratby.law/agentic-ai-payments-consent-regulation/); [Vixio](https://vixio.com/regulatory-news/pc-vixio-view-fcas-payments-priorities-offer-clarity-rather-novelty-except-ai)

### Inferences
- [I] Treat every agent-initiated payment as needing a "consent artefact" that would satisfy dynamic linking: the user's passkey/SCA signs a hash of {amount, currency, payee account, payee name, FX rate, fees, purpose}; if the agent changes any field after signing, the signature no longer verifies and the payment is blocked. This mirrors both SCA dynamic linking and PSD3/PSR's "fraudster-modified = unauthorised".
- [I] Build a "remittance disclosure card" (fees, FX rate, amount received, delivery date, 30-minute cancel window, how to dispute) shown before every L2/L3 cross-border execution, and implement a cancel window in the sandbox. Cheap to build, directly scoreable.
- [I] Anti-scam guardian idea fits the UK/EU direction exactly: regimes push liability onto PSPs, so banks want upstream controls that catch coerced payments (impersonation, "safe account", romance, fake tuition portal). The guardian should produce evidence (warnings shown, VoP result, user acknowledgement) that would matter in a reimbursement dispute.
- [I] PSR's "human support, not only chatbots" means the agent must always expose a "talk to a human / freeze my account" exit.

### Gaps
- Could not confirm whether PSD3/PSR is published in the OJ as of 16 Oct 2026, nor exact article numbers.
- Did not verify current CFPB status of the 30-minute rule beyond secondary source; CFPB 2025–2026 changes to Reg E remittance rules not found.
- US 1% excise tax on cash remittances (reported for 2026) not researched; low relevance for account-funded student payments.
- Whether the UK PSR has been consolidated into the FCA by Oct 2026 not confirmed.

## Q3. Data protection for student documents (bank statements, passports, admission letters): GDPR, China PIPL & cross-border transfer, Vietnam PDPL

### Takeaway
All three regimes treat financial data as high-risk: China PIPL classes financial accounts as sensitive and gates exports by volume and purpose, but the 2024 Provisions exempt transfers necessary to perform a contract with the individual (explicitly incl. cross-border fund transfers, payments, account opening, visas, exams); Vietnam's PDPL (Law 91/2025, in force 1 Jan 2026, with Decree 356) makes financial data sensitive and requires transfer impact assessments; GDPR requires minimisation, DPIA and Art. 22 safeguards for automated decisions. Practical answer for a 72h build: minimise (VCs/selective disclosure instead of raw statements), keep PII in-region, send only redacted text to any foreign LLM, and log consent per purpose.

### Cited Findings
- [F2] China's Provisions on Promoting and Regulating Cross-Border Data Flows (22 Mar 2024): transfers necessary to enter into or perform a contract with the individual are exempt, with examples incl. cross-border shopping, fund transfers, payments, account opening, air/hotel booking, visa applications, taking exams; <100,000 individuals' non-sensitive PI per calendar year exempt; 100k–1M non-sensitive or <10,000 sensitive → standard contract or certification; >1M non-sensitive or >10,000 sensitive → CAC security assessment; CIIOs excluded from de minimis; "important data" always needs assessment — [Hogan Lovells](https://www.hoganlovells.com/en/publications/china-finalizes-its-provisions-to-promote-and-regulate-cross-border-data-transfers); [Fangda](https://fangdalaw.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/China-promulgated-the-Provisions-on-Standardizing-and-Promoting-Cross-Border-Data-Flows_Fangda_20240324_Clean-005-SD0324-618883-v1.pdf)
- [F2] CAC/SAMR Measures for Certification of cross-border PI transfer issued 14 Oct 2025, effective 1 Jan 2026; first three accredited certification bodies named 30 Dec 2025 — [CMS](https://cms.law/en/chn/legal-updates/china-issues-measures-for-the-certification-of-the-cross-border-transfer-of-personal-information); [Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/china/security/1701278/china-finalizes-certification-route-for-cross-border-data-transfer)
- [F2] PBOC and five other departments issued Compliance Guidelines for Promoting and Regulating Cross-Border Data Flows in the Financial Sector on 17 Apr 2025, listing scenarios and data items that may flow abroad (list not retrieved) — [Pakistan Today](https://profit.pakistantoday.com.pk/2025/04/17/china-issues-new-rules-to-regulate-cross-border-financial-data-flow); [Clifford Chance, Jun 2025](https://www.cliffordchance.com/content/dam/cliffordchance/briefings/2025/06/Client%20Briefing%20-%20PBOC%20Data%20and%20Cyber%20Security%20Measures%20-%20Practical%20Compliance%20Guide%20for%20Financial%20I.pdf)
- [PK] PIPL Art. 28 lists financial accounts among sensitive personal information (separate consent, necessity); Art. 38–39 govern cross-border provision incl. separate consent — [PIPL English translation, DigiChina](https://digichina.stanford.edu/work/translation-personal-information-protection-law-of-the-peoples-republic-of-china-effective-nov-1-2021/) (not re-fetched)
- [F2] Vietnam: Law on Personal Data Protection No. 91/2025/QH15 enacted 26 Jun 2025; Decree 356 (implementing, replacing Decree 13/2023) effective 1 Jan 2026; cross-border transfers (incl. storing on foreign cloud, sending to offshore platforms) generally require a filed transfer impact assessment (TIA) with exemptions; sensitive data widened to include financial information, biometrics, precise location, account credentials; fines up to 5% of prior-year revenue for cross-border transfer violations — [Vietnam Briefing](https://www.vietnam-briefing.com/news/vietnam-personal-data-protection-regulation-decree-356.html); [Tilleke & Gibbins](https://www.tilleke.com/insights/vietnams-new-personal-data-protection-law-a-closer-look/14/); [Rajah & Tann](https://www.rajahtannasia.com/viewpoints/new-decree-implementing-the-law-on-personal-data-protection-passed/)
- [PK] GDPR: Art. 5(1)(c) data minimisation; Art. 22 right not to be subject to solely automated decisions with legal/similarly significant effects (relevant to credit identity); Art. 35 DPIA for high-risk processing; Chapter V restricts transfers outside the EEA — [EUR-Lex GDPR](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/oj) (not re-fetched)
- [F2] NFRA (June 2026) bars using PII (names, ID numbers, mobile numbers, bank account numbers) to train or fine-tune generative AI models; institutions must desensitise datasets and prove exclusion via data lineage — [AML Network](https://amlnetwork.org/aml-news/china-ai-finance-rules-nfras-2026-bank-and-insurer-framework/)

### Inferences
- [I] Proof-of-funds VC idea is a privacy win to emphasise: the bank issues a signed claim ("balance ≥ X CNY/VND on date D, holder = DID") and the university/embassy verifies it; no raw statement leaves the bank. Use selective disclosure (SD-JWT or BBS+) and short validity.
- [I] For the 72h build: process documents locally/in-region; send the LLM only redacted fields (mask passport number, account numbers to last 4); store originals encrypted with per-user keys so "right to delete" = key destruction (crypto-shredding), which keeps hash-only on-chain logs compatible with erasure.
- [I] Log a consent record per purpose (e.g., "share admission letter with Bank X for FX purchase, valid 30 days") and show it in the UI; judges from a Chinese bank will recognise "separate consent" for sensitive PI and cross-border.
- [I] For the departure/offboarding agent, data deletion, consent withdrawal and mandate revocation are core features, not afterthoughts.

## Q4. EU AI Act: timeline after the 2026 "Digital Omnibus", creditworthiness as high-risk, transparency duties for chatbots/agents

### Takeaway
As of Oct 2026 the AI Act chatbot-disclosure duty (Art. 50) applies from 2 Aug 2026 and was not deferred, while stand-alone Annex III high-risk obligations (incl. creditworthiness scoring of natural persons) moved from 2 Aug 2026 to 2 Dec 2027 via the AI Omnibus (Parliament voted 16 Jun 2026; Council approved; OJ publication reported July 2026 by one source). Portable credit identity = high-risk by design; anti-scam guardian sits in the fraud-detection carve-out; any user-facing agent must disclose it is AI.

### Cited Findings
- [F2] Provisional AI Omnibus deal reached 7 May 2026: stand-alone Annex III high-risk obligations move from 2 Aug 2026 to 2 Dec 2027; product-embedded Annex I to 2 Aug 2028; new Art. 5 prohibition on AI-generated non-consensual intimate imagery/CSAM — [Gibson Dunn](https://www.gibsondunn.com/eu-ai-act-omnibus-agreement); [Sidley](https://datamatters.sidley.com/2026/06/22/eu-lawmakers-reach-provisional-agreement-to-delay-key-eu-ai-act-obligations/); [Hogan Lovells](https://www.hoganlovells.com/en/publications/eu-legislators-agree-to-delay-for-highrisk-ai-rules)
- [F2] European Parliament adopted it 16 Jun 2026 (423–57–174); Council final approval reported around end-June/1 Jul 2026; one source says it was signed 8 Jul, published in the OJ 24 Jul and entered into force 27 Jul 2026, another cites it as Regulation (EU) 2026/1744 — publication NOT confirmed from EUR-Lex this session — [iubenda](https://www.iubenda.com/en/blog/ai-omnibus-parliament-adoption-june-2026); [FPF](https://fpf.org/?p=250371); [Compliance Hub](https://compliancehub.wiki/eu-ai-act-article-50-transparency-digital-omnibus-2026/)
- [F2] Article 50 transparency obligations were not deferred: chatbot/virtual-assistant deployers must inform users they are interacting with AI from 2 Aug 2026; fines up to EUR 15m or 3% of worldwide turnover; watermarking for systems already on the market reportedly extended to 2 Dec 2026 (sources differ); Commission published a voluntary Code of Practice on transparency of AI-generated content in June 2026 — [Compliance Hub](https://compliancehub.wiki/eu-ai-act-article-50-transparency-digital-omnibus-2026/); [Dastra](https://www.dastra.eu/en/blog/ai-act-transparency-rules-what-changes-august-2-2026/60164)
- [PK] Annex III point 5(b) lists AI used to evaluate creditworthiness or establish credit scores of natural persons as high-risk, "with the exception of AI systems used for the purpose of detecting financial fraud"; high-risk providers/deployers need risk management, logging (Art. 12), human oversight (Art. 14), transparency to deployers (Art. 13) — [EUR-Lex Regulation (EU) 2024/1689](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2024/1689/oj) (not re-fetched)

### Inferences
- [I] Portable credit identity: if it scores students for credit, it is Annex III high-risk in the EU (from Dec 2027) and touches GDPR Art. 22; present it as "user-held verified data (VCs of rent/utility/tuition payment history) that a lender's own model consumes", so the hackathon product is a data-portability layer, not the scorer. That keeps the agent out of the high-risk box and makes the permission story simpler.
- [I] Anti-scam guardian benefits from the fraud-detection carve-out, but still needs human override, explanation of why a payment was paused, and Art. 50 disclosure.
- [I] Even though the judges are Chinese/WeBank, EU AI Act Art. 12/14 (logging, human oversight) maps 1:1 onto China NFRA requirements (human oversight, emergency shutdown) — cite both to show a global design.

## Q5. What financial regulators and standard setters say about AI agents in payments/finance (2025–2026)

### Takeaway
2026 is the year regulators moved from generic AI principles to agent-specific runtime controls: MAS published SAFR (3 Jul 2026), a runtime checkpoint with agent identity, controls repository, disposition engine (deny/escalate/auto-execute/observe) and tamper-evident audit log, then issued final AI Risk Management Guidelines (7 Oct 2026); the FSB consulted (10 Jun 2026) on sound practices incl. prohibited-action lists, per-agent identifiers, human approval/dual authorisation above thresholds and restricting direct agent access to payment systems; China's NFRA (18 Jun 2026) mandates risk tiering, human oversight and emergency shutdown for high-risk uses incl. fund transfers and credit approval; HKMA's GenAI Sandbox++ first cohort (27 Aug 2026) is focused on agentic AI incl. payments. The permission-tier model below is essentially SAFR + FSB + NFRA in miniature.

### Cited Findings
- [F2] MAS "Safeguards for Agentic Finance at Runtime" (SAFR) white paper, 3 Jul 2026, under BuildFin.ai, extending the Project MindForge AI risk toolkit: governance checkpoints between each agent decision and execution, via a "governance envelope" (structured record of intended action, why, and supporting evidence); four components: agent identity (verified against registry), controls repository (internal policy, regulation, product rules and user-provided mandates), disposition engine (deny / escalate / auto-execute / observe), tamper-evident append-only audit log; use cases incl. agent-assisted payments and treasury "within predefined mandates" — [Rajah & Tann](https://www.rajahtannasia.com/viewpoints/mas-publishes-white-paper-for-fis-on-developing-safeguards-for-ai-agents-in-finance/); [Digital Policy Alert](https://digitalpolicyalert.org/event/41671-monetary-authority-published-white-paper-on-safeguards-for-agentic-finance-at-runtime); [Fintech Global](https://fintech.global/2026/07/06/mas-moves-to-rein-in-autonomous-ai-agents-in-finance/)

## Q6. Agent security threats: OWASP LLM Top 10 (2025), OWASP Top 10 for Agentic Applications (2026), injection, tool abuse, memory poisoning, identity spoofing, real incidents

### Takeaway
The dominant threat for a money-moving agent is indirect prompt injection that hijacks the agent's goal via content it reads (invoice, admission letter, email, web page), amplified by excessive agency; 2026 produced real losses (Grok/Bankrbot, ~USD 150–200k via a Morse-encoded prompt) and 2025 produced a zero-click enterprise exploit (EchoLeak). OWASP's 2026 agentic list adds identity/privilege abuse, memory poisoning, inter-agent spoofing and human-agent trust exploitation (approval fatigue) — all directly relevant to co-signer and autopilot designs.

### Cited Findings
- [F2] OWASP Top 10 for LLM Applications 2025: LLM01 Prompt Injection, LLM02 Sensitive Information Disclosure, LLM03 Supply Chain, LLM04 Data and Model Poisoning, LLM05 Improper Output Handling, LLM06 Excessive Agency, LLM07 System Prompt Leakage, LLM08 Vector and Embedding Weaknesses, LLM09 Misinformation, LLM10 Unbounded Consumption; excessive agency example: support bot tricked into issuing unauthorised refunds — [Invicti](https://invicti.com/blog/web-security/owasp-top-10-risks-llm-security-2025); [Outpost24](https://outpost24.com/blog/owasp-top-10-llm-risks-explained/)
- [F] OWASP Top 10 for Agentic Applications for 2026 published 9 Dec 2025 by the OWASP GenAI Security Project, 100+ contributors — [OWASP GenAI](https://genai.owasp.org/resource/owasp-top-10-for-agentic-applications-for-2026)

- [F2] Same incident: attacker first sent the agent's wallet an NFT that unlocked broader transfer permissions; Bankrbot executed without further verification; ~80% of funds later returned; classified under prompt injection + excessive agency — [Giskard](https://giskard.ai/knowledge/how-grok-got-prompt-injected-an-x-user-drained-150-000-from-an-ai-wallet); [Cointelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/news/grok-bankr-wallet-free-nft-prompt-injection)

- [F] EchoLeak (CVE-2025-32711): zero-click indirect prompt injection in Microsoft 365 Copilot; a single crafted email retrieved later into context enabled remote unauthenticated data exfiltration, bypassing the XPIA classifier and link redaction; patched; no confirmed in-the-wild exploitation; suggested mitigations: prompt partitioning, input/output filtering, provenance-based access control, strict CSP — [arXiv 2509.10540](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.10540v1); [BleepingComputer](https://bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/zero-click-ai-data-leak-flaw-uncovered-in-microsoft-365-copilot)

### Inferences
- [I] Grok/Bankrbot lesson for judges: the agent had (a) a permission that could be expanded by an external event (NFT), (b) no deterministic limit or payee allow-list, (c) no out-of-band confirmation. Each tier control below answers one of these.

## Q7. Industry patterns for delegated authority (mandates, agent tokens, grants, session keys, step-up, cooling-off, dual control, trusted contacts, kill switches)

### Takeaway
The industry has converged on "signed, scoped, revocable mandates + per-transaction proof of user intent": Google's AP2 (intent/cart/payment mandates as W3C Verifiable Credentials), Visa Intelligent Commerce and Mastercard Agent Pay (agent-specific network tokens with spend limits/merchant allow-lists plus passkey authentication), Interledger Open Payments (interactive GNAP grants for outgoing payments, rotatable/revocable tokens) and ERC-4337/ERC-7715 session keys (per-period spending limits enforced by the account). A hackathon build can copy the pattern without integrating any network.

### Cited Findings
- [F2] AP2 (announced 16 Sep 2025, extension of A2A, works with MCP, 60+ partners): Intent Mandate captures the user's task and constraints (e.g., price ceiling, timing) for delegated tasks; Cart Mandate locks items, price and terms on user approval (sources differ on merchant co-signature) and can be generated automatically when Intent Mandate conditions are met; Payment Mandate signals to networks/issuers that an AI agent was involved; mandates are W3C VCs (tamper-evident signed JSON) — [Vellum](https://www.vellum.ai/blog/googles-ap2-a-new-protocol-for-ai-agent-payments); [Worldline docs](https://docs.connect.worldline-solutions.com/documentation/ConnectAI/agent-payments-protocol-ap2); [arXiv 2510.25819](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.25819)
- [F2] Visa Intelligent Commerce (introduced Apr 2025): agent-specific revocable tokens tied to Visa fraud tools and passkey authentication confirming a verified agent on a registered device; Trusted Agent Protocol with per-transaction signed intents — [FinSignals](https://finsignals.substack.com/p/agentic-payments-part-2); [eco.com](https://eco.com/support/en/articles/15192003)
- [F2] Mastercard Agent Pay: "Agentic Tokens" scoped with spend limits, merchant allow-lists and transaction categories; layers of tokenisation, biometric passkey authentication and "Verifiable Intent" record plus "Know Your Agent"; live authenticated agentic transactions reported in Hong Kong (27 Mar) and Thailand (7 Apr) using Mastercard Payment Passkeys (year not stated in summary; rollout claims vary) — [eco.com](https://eco.com/support/en/articles/14730449); [Stellagent](https://stellagent.ai/insights/visa-mastercard-agentic-commerce-strategy); [TechInformed](https://techinformed.com/visa-opens-one-integration-for-ai-agent-payments/)

## Q8. Recommended permission-tier model, policy-engine logic and tamper-evident audit evidence

### Takeaway
Use four tiers (L0 inform, L1 prepare, L2 execute within a signed mandate, L3 step-up/multi-party) plus a hard "never" list, enforced by a deterministic policy engine outside the LLM that returns SAFR-style dispositions (deny / escalate / auto-execute / observe); every proposal and decision goes into a hash-chained, signed, append-only log whose Merkle roots are periodically anchored on a permissioned chain. This directly mirrors MAS SAFR, FSB agentic practices, NFRA risk tiering/emergency shutdown, EU AI Act Art. 12/14 and OWASP "least agency".

### Cited Findings
- [F2] SAFR: governance envelope per action; agent identity check; controls repository incl. user mandates; disposition engine with deny/escalate/auto-execute/observe; tamper-evident append-only audit log — [Rajah & Tann](https://www.rajahtannasia.com/viewpoints/mas-publishes-white-paper-for-fis-on-developing-safeguards-for-ai-agents-in-finance/)
- [F2] FSB: prohibited-action lists, per-agent identifier, human approval/dual authorisation above thresholds, restrict direct access to payment systems, audit trails — [TechInformed](https://techinformed.com/fsb-calls-for-tighter-controls-on-agentic-ai-in-finance/)
- [F2] NFRA: graded risk classification, human oversight at critical decision points and mandatory emergency shutdown for high-risk uses incl. fund transfers — [AML Network](https://amlnetwork.org/aml-news/china-ai-finance-rules-nfras-2026-bank-and-insurer-framework/)
- [F2] OWASP agentic principles: least agency; short-lived task-scoped credentials; treat retrieved content as untrusted; kill switch; show raw action to human and log what was displayed — [Cycode](https://cycode.com/blog/owasp-top-10-agentic-applications/)
- [PK] EU AI Act Art. 12 (automatic logging) and Art. 14 (human oversight incl. ability to stop the system) for high-risk systems — [EUR-Lex 2024/1689](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2024/1689/oj)

### Inferences

#### Tier model [I]

| Tier | What the agent may do | Examples (Topic C ideas) | Controls | Who/what authorises |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **L0 Inform** (read-only) | Read balances, FX quotes, deadlines, rules; explain; compare | "When is tuition due, how much in CNY/VND at today's rate, what docs do I need for the FX purchase?" | Read-only scoped token; no write tools in the toolset; PII redaction before LLM; AI disclosure banner (AI Act Art. 50 / China labeling); answers cite sources; "not advice" framing | Session login |
| **L1 Prepare** (draft, nothing leaves) | Extract data from docs, fill forms, draft payment instruction / FX declaration / proof-of-funds VC request, propose mandate | Parse admission letter + invoice into a structured payment draft; prepare outbound-FX paperwork pack; draft offboarding checklist | Documents processed as data only (instruction/data separation, injection classifier, hidden-text stripping); extracted fields schema-validated; side-by-side diff (extracted vs source highlight); every field gets a provenance tag (user-typed / bank-verified / extracted-from-untrusted-doc) | User reviews; nothing is sent |
| **L2 Execute within mandate** | Execute payments/actions that fully satisfy a user-signed mandate | Monthly allowance CNY X to verified child account; tuition to the school's VoP-verified account up to invoice amount; renewing a proof-of-funds VC | Mandate VC signature valid and unexpired; payee on allow-list AND live VoP = match; amount ≤ per-tx and period caps; velocity/count limits; FX quota remaining and docs attached; sanctions pre-screen clear; no taint (no parameter came from untrusted content this session); risk score < threshold; idempotency key; single-use payment token bound to `hash(mandate_id, payee, amount, nonce)`; post-execution notification + cancel window (e.g., 30 min, mirroring Reg E) | Policy engine auto-executes; user notified |
| **L3 Step-up / multi-party** | Anything outside the mandate or any risk flag | New payee or changed bank details; VoP close/no match; amount over cap; first payment to new corridor; scam signals (urgency, "safe account", crypto, gift cards, impersonation of school/police/bank); sanctions possible hit; creating/changing a mandate; any credit application; sharing documents with a new party | SCA with dynamic linking (passkey signs the exact amount+payee+FX hash; raw action displayed, not summary); optional co-signer (parent/trusted contact) for > threshold or scam-flagged; cooling-off (e.g., 24 h) for first payment to a new payee above a small amount; ops review for sanctions hits; dual authorisation for mandate changes | User SCA (+ co-signer / ops) |
| **Never (L-X)** | Hard-coded denials regardless of instructions | Agent editing its own mandate/limits; adding payees without L3; paying to crypto off-ramps or personal accounts of "agents"; splitting payments to evade FX quota or thresholds; acting on instructions found inside documents/emails/web pages; exporting raw ID/bank docs to non-allow-listed parties | Deterministic deny + alert | Nobody (requires product change) |

#### Kill switches [I]
- User "freeze" button (revokes all agent tokens, cancels pending, sets all mandates to suspended); guardian/co-signer freeze; operator global kill (circuit breaker on anomaly rate, e.g., N denials in M minutes or model outage → fail closed to L0). Log each kill test as sandbox evidence (NFRA "emergency shutdown").

#### How the policy engine decides [I]
1. LLM agent never holds payment credentials; it emits a **governance envelope**: `{envelope_id, agent_id, agent_version, model_id, user_id, action_type, params, requested_tier, rationale, evidence_refs[doc hashes], provenance_tags per param}`.
2. Engine (deterministic, policy-as-code, e.g., JSON rules / OPA) loads controls: regulatory rules (FX quota, anti-structuring, sanctions), product rules, user mandate.
3. Ordered checks: identity (agent registered, version allow-listed) → hard-deny list → mandate match → payee verification (VoP/alignment) → limits/velocity → FX quota & documents → sanctions pre-screen → taint check (any param from untrusted source ⇒ escalate) → risk score (scam heuristics + anomaly) → disposition.
4. Disposition: **deny** (with reason code), **escalate** (L3 flow with the specific step-up required), **auto-execute** (L2), **observe** (allowed but flagged for post-review, e.g., first use of a new feature).
5. Optional independent "checker" model re-reads source docs and the envelope and can only lower the disposition (never raise it).

#### What to write to the tamper-evident log [I]
Per entry: `seq, timestamp (trusted), entry_type (proposal/decision/approval/execution/cancel/kill/config_change), envelope_id, agent_id + version + model hash + prompt-template hash, pseudonymous user_id, action_type, params (amount, currency, payee account hash, payee name), requested_tier, granted_tier, disposition, rule_ids fired + reason codes, mandate_id + mandate hash, input document hashes, provenance/taint flags, VoP result, sanctions-screen result + list version, FX quota before/after, risk score + features, what was displayed to the approver (screen hash), SCA assertion hash (WebAuthn signature), co-signer ids, execution reference, prev_hash, entry_hash = H(prev_hash || canonical_json(entry)), signer signature`.
- No raw PII on chain; only hashes/IDs. Raw records encrypted off-chain with per-user keys (supports deletion via key destruction).
- Every N entries or T minutes: compute Merkle root, anchor it on a permissioned consortium chain (a WeBank-aligned option is FISCO BCOS — [I/PK], not researched this session) with tx id written back to the log; verifier script recomputes chain + root to prove no tampering.
- Sandbox evidence package: (a) scenario runs per tier incl. red-team cases (injected invoice with new IBAN → escalate/deny; "police/safe-account" scam → co-signer + cooling-off; over-cap → step-up; structuring attempt → deny; memory-poisoning attempt → deny; kill-switch drill → all tokens revoked < X s); (b) log extract + verification output; (c) metrics: actions by tier and disposition, attacks blocked, false-positive escalations, median approval time, time-to-kill.

### Gaps
- No regulator prescribes specific thresholds (caps, cooling-off hours) for agent tiers; values above are design choices to tune.
- Permissioned-chain anchoring is not required by any rule found; present it as evidence integrity, not compliance.

4. **Excessive agency / over-broad, long-lived credentials (LLM06/ASI03)** → LLM holds no payment credentials; short-lived single-use tokens bound to mandate hash; least-agency toolset per tier → log: token issuance/expiry, scope.
5. **Mandate tampering or self-escalation (agent edits limits/payees)** → mandates signed by user passkey, read-only to agent, changes only via L3 dual control → log: mandate hash per decision, change approvals.
6. **Memory/context poisoning (ASI06)** → no autonomous writes to long-term memory of payees/limits; memory entries provenance-tagged and expire; payees only from verified registry → log: memory write events + source.
7. **Hallucinated regulatory/FX facts (LLM09) leading to wrong paperwork or quota breach** → rules and quotas come from a versioned rules table, not the model; answers cite sources; FX rate from quoted API with timestamp → log: rule-table version, quote id.
8. **AML/sanctions miss or structuring to evade FX quota/thresholds** → pre-screen + bank screening, anti-splitting rule across a rolling window, purpose documentation → log: screening list version + result, cumulative-amount check.
9. **Forged documents / synthetic proof-of-funds** → prefer bank-issued VCs over uploaded PDFs; verify issuer signatures; flag PDFs whose metadata/fonts are inconsistent → log: VC issuer DID + verification result.
10. **Privacy leakage & unlawful cross-border transfer (PIPL/PDPL/GDPR)** → redaction before LLM, in-region processing, per-purpose consent, retention limits, crypto-shredding → log: consent id, redaction applied, data-transfer destination.
11. **Human-agent trust exploitation / approval fatigue (ASI09)** → show raw action and risk reasons, rate-limit approvals, require typed confirmation for high-risk, rotate co-signer prompts → log: screen hash shown, time-to-approve.
12. **Identity spoofing of user, co-signer or agent (SIM swap, deepfake voice, fake agent) (ASI03/ASI07)** → passkeys/WebAuthn (phishing-resistant), registered agent identities with signed envelopes, no SMS-only approval for L3 → log: authenticator id, agent signature.
13. **Tool/supply-chain compromise (malicious MCP server, poisoned FX-rate API) (ASI04/LLM03)** → pinned/allow-listed tools, signed tool manifests, output validation, egress allow-list → log: tool version/hash per call.
14. **Duplicate or cascading execution (retries, loops, multi-agent fan-out) (ASI08)** → idempotency keys, max actions per mandate period, circuit breaker → log: idempotency key, breaker trips.
15. **Stale mandates after life events (graduation, departure, loss of phone)** → mandate expiry by default, offboarding agent revokes all, inactivity auto-suspend → log: revocation events.
16. **Unfair/opaque credit decisions (portable credit identity) — EU AI Act high-risk, GDPR Art. 22** → product only conveys user-held verified data; lender decides; explanations and contestation path → log: data shared, consent, decision source.
17. **Disclosure failures (fees, FX margin, cancellation/dispute rights)** → mandatory disclosure card before execution, cancel window → log: disclosure version shown + timestamp.
18. **Audit-log tampering or repudiation** → hash chain + signatures + periodic anchoring + independent verifier script → log: anchor tx ids, verification report.
19. **Model outage/rogue behaviour (ASI10)** → fail closed to L0, global kill switch, behavioural baseline alerts → log: kill/fail-closed events and recovery time.
